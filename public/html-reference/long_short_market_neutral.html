The Definition

Market neutral funds seek to profit from both increasing and decreasing prices in one or more markets, while attempting to completely avoid any specific market risk. This is achieved by balancing long and short positions to maintain zero net exposure.

Legal Framework

Investment Approach

Simultaneous long positions in undervalued assets and short positions in overvalued counterparts within the same sector.

The Mechanism

Identification

Quantitative and qualitative analysis to find pricing discrepancies between related securities.

Pairing

Securities are paired to cancel out industry-wide movements, leaving only the relative performance.

Execution

Leverage is often applied to amplify the narrow spreads captured between the long and short legs.

Regulatory Warning & Taxation

Taxation of AIFs depends on the specific domicile and the legal status of the investor. Returns may be treated as capital gains or professional income. Investors are strongly advised to consult with a sovereign tax advisor before allocation.

Systemic Risks

  • • Leverage risks amplifying small losses.
  • • Operational risk in short-selling mechanics.
  • • Model failure during "Black Swan" correlation events.

Strategic Rewards

  • • Capital preservation during market downturns.
  • • Lower volatility compared to equity benchmarks.
  • • Diversification of traditional 60/40 portfolios.

Is this for you?

Designed for institutional entities and ultra-high-net-worth individuals seeking a non-directional exposure with a minimum investment horizon of 3-5 years.
